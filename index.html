Four costs hide between the board and your bank

Each is small on its own. Together they decide which buyer is actually best.

5–15%

Long-haul freight

A truck to a distant terminal, plus the empty return trip, can cancel out a price premium.

Up to 3%

Transit shrinkage

Perishable crops lose weight and quality on long journeys, most of all in summer heat.

1–1.5%

Cess and handling

Fees and loading charges differ by market and rarely appear next to the rate.

Weeks

Delayed payment

Cash tied up in settlement is cash you can’t put into the next crop.

Common questions

Are these live market prices?

No. This is a concept demo. All prices, volumes and markets are illustrative sample data.

How is net return calculated?

Gross value at the advertised price, minus freight (distance × rate × tonnes), market cess, handling, and an estimated transit shrinkage that grows with distance and how perishable the crop is.

Can I change the assumptions?

Yes. The calculator lets you change load size, freight rate, handling charge, shrinkage, cess and the empty return trip to match your own quotes.

Why does a nearer buyer sometimes win?

A small price premium at a distant market is often wiped out by freight and shrinkage, especially for perishable crops like tomato.
